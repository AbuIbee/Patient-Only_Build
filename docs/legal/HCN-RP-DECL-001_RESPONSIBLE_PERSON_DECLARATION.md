### Mandatory legal notes for implementation, not part of signer's attestation
1. **HIPAA personal representative authority depends on state/other applicable law**; a self-attestation alone is not verification under 45 CFR 164.514(h). Contact only is not full proxy. See HHS guidance: https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/personal-representatives/index.html and https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/faq/how-would-a-covered-entity-know-if-someone-were-a-personal-representative/index.html.
2. **Do not use this document as the legal instrument appointing a healthcare agent.** In North Carolina, for example, the statutory healthcare power-of-attorney instrument calls for two qualified witnesses and notarization; this declaration is merely evidence of the claimant's representations. See https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_32A/GS_32A-25.1.html.
3. E-SIGN, 15 USC 7001, generally recognizes electronic signatures for applicable transactions but does not nullify independent substantive legal requirements, and has specific consent/retention rules.
4. 28 USC 1746 describes unsworn declarations in particular matters governed by federal law; **DO NOT assert that including "penalty of perjury" in an ordinary private website automatically invokes 28 USC 1746 or creates an enforceable criminal penalty**. Counsel must approve actual applicable perjury wording and jurisdiction.
5. No declaration can completely indemnify Haneen against its own failure to verify authority, safeguard information or comply with applicable law. Do not make a waiver of these duties a precondition to receiving legally mandated access.
